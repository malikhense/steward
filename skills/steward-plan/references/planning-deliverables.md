# Plans that can be lived

Context establishes current reality; the Goal establishes the destination; the Plan connects them. Keep transition logic inside the Plan. Find blockers before scheduling: wake baseline, obligations, injury/ability, travel, setup, cooking time, budget, household control and recovery. If the desired slot is not feasible now, research and prepare the transition plus a workable current action. Keep time for unexpected events and rest.

A compact Plan can show Goal → Current → Plan → Why. The final usable deliverables carry all execution detail. For each action resolve what, when/trigger, where, how, duration, equipment/materials, completion and what happens next. Operational precision must follow available evidence: label an estimate or conditional slot instead of inventing certainty.

## Repetition and calibration

Attach every recurring action to an existing or appropriate Routine and a trigger/Block. Specify order, timing, frequency, needed equipment and tracking only when useful. Discover owned resources before shopping. Account for conditional steps and other people sharing the space.

Meet current ability. Include a minimum/floor and normal/target version; add a ceiling or progression when useful and safe. Explain the trigger for moving up, down or stopping. A short check-in can distinguish too easy, right, too hard, painful or skipped. Skipping is evidence about fit; investigate friction and adjust, without blame. Do not interpret silence as success.

## Deliverable completion

Use these as applicable completion criteria, not a demand to create every artifact. Apply each detail to the requested purpose: nutrition estimates matter for nutrition targets or dietary adequacy; exact products, local prices, and store selection matter for budgeted or purchase-ready shopping. An ordinary recipe list still needs servings, coherent ingredient quantities, and usable preparation, but need not become a product-research project.

| Deliverable | What makes it usable |
|---|---|
| Exercise | Exact warm-up and sequence, sets/reps/rest, form cues, appropriate modifications, progression, clear cardio intensity/duration; verified quality demos for unfamiliar movements |
| Meals | Selected real meals, substitutions, exact portions/servings, preparation/storage instructions; for nutrition plans, ingredient-based estimates and adequate overall energy as well as the target nutrient |
| Groceries | Meal quantities aggregated across the plan, pantry amounts subtracted only when known, package sizes rounded up for purchasing; for budgeted or purchase-ready shopping, exact suitable products, store/location, prices and cart/list status |
| Schedule | Dates, local timezone, start/end or clear anchor-relative trigger, travel/setup, dependencies, recovery/slack, planned-versus-live status |
| Routine | Trigger, order, timing/duration, frequency, resources, useful recording, fallback |
| Budget/shopping | Line items, quantities, unit/pack cost, payer, existing resources, upfront and recurring totals; affordability and uncertainty |
| Message | Correct person/channel, relationship-specific voice, concrete useful content and authorization status |
| Artifact | Actually created in the requested format, opens correctly, readable layout, accessible location; a description of a PDF is not a PDF |

When a tracker or review depends on arithmetic, supply working formulas, a runnable calculation or an available automated review path. A blank data table plus “compare weekly averages” leaves setup and repeated calculation unfinished. Verify the calculation with sample data, keeping samples separate from actual user records.

Read labels and recipe serving definitions. Recompute nutrition after substitutions and portion changes; do not attach unmodified source totals to a modified recipe. Check ingredient totals against servings and plan length. Label approximate nutritional values and uncertain requirements. Feedback can refine energy needs; it does not excuse a visibly incomplete day of food.

For movement instruction, solve the knowledge gap with a good demonstration or concise instructions. Decorative stick figures do not establish correct technique. If a demo is needed, find the exact relevant link rather than sending the user away to search.

## One coherent cost view

Separate upfront cash outlay from consumption cost and recurring run rate. Show useful weekly/monthly/annual views, using explicit assumptions (e.g. 52 weeks / 12 months). Do not add alternate time views together. Show owned resources as zero incremental purchase, not free lifetime consumption. Household-paid and user-paid shares must reconcile to total, with unknown responsibility marked unresolved rather than zero.

When comparing options, calculate the net cost over the user's actual decision horizon, such as the remaining contract or planned period of use. Include setup/exit fees, overlap and timing of payments. State that horizon's gain or loss explicitly before longer-term projections; a favorable annual view must not hide a loss or cash burden during the period the user is deciding about.

Include products, quantities, pack prices, expected usage, shipping/tax/fees where known and price date/source. Estimated totals are estimates; when live prices require a capable service, use capability discovery rather than stopping at “unavailable.” Do not call a total complete if it excludes an unresolved major cost.

## Consistency pass

Before delivery, verify schedule arithmetic and dependency timing; reconcile all daily/weekly quantities and totals; ensure every repeated action has a home; check substitutions against constraints; open/render requested artifacts. Match the schedule, guide, grocery list and routine to the same Plan version. Audit remaining user work and complete reasonable missing implementation steps.
