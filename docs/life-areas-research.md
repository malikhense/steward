# Life-area frameworks: evidence for an editable starter menu

Researched 16 September 2026. Scope: primary-source taxonomy comparison for a personal planning product.

## Finding

Use life areas as a coverage aid that the person can edit, not a universal diagnosis or required set of goals. These four established frameworks serve different purposes and use different levels of detail. The retrieved sources do not establish one optimal number of areas, validate a universal taxonomy, or justify a composite personal “life score.”

## Comparison

| Framework and purpose | Exact source categories | Coverage strengths and product gaps (analysis) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **SAMHSA Eight Dimensions of Wellness**, wellness and behavioral-health recovery | Emotional, environmental, financial, intellectual, occupational, physical, social, spiritual. | Broad coverage beyond career, with explicit emotional health and environment. Leisure, caregiving, and relationship types are not distinct top-level labels. Its recovery context should not be mistaken for validation of a goal-planning taxonomy. [SAMHSA community guide](https://store.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/sma16-4957.pdf), [official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tDzQdRvLAfM). |
| **Full Focus LifeScore / LifeFocus**, commercial life planning and goal selection | Body, mind, spirit, love, family, community, money, work, hobbies. | Separates relationships and includes recreation. Home/environment and civic participation are not distinct areas. Current product descriptions treat love as the most significant relationship, family as close people, and community as friendships/groups. [Current LifeScore](https://assessments.fullfocus.co/lifescore/), [LifeFocus kit](https://fullfocusstore.com/products/lifefocus-kit). |
| **Stanford Life Design / Designing Your Life dashboard**, reflection on current balance | Health, work, play, love; Stanford's worksheet also provides **Other**. | Small, approachable overview, but money, housing, learning, and specific relationships can disappear inside broad headings unless added. The Stanford worksheet explicitly invites adding an area that feels missing. [Stanford 2024 workbook, Balance Assessment, printed p. 4](https://lifedesignlab.stanford.edu/s/June-2024-Virtual-Studio-Workbook.pdf), [authors' workbook page](https://designingyour.life/designing-your-life-workbook/). |
| **OECD Better Life Index**, comparison of well-being across countries | Housing, income, jobs, community, education, environment, civic engagement, health, life satisfaction, safety, work-life balance. | Reveals structural conditions easily missed by personal productivity systems. Country indicators are not personal action categories: life satisfaction is an outcome, and safety or housing may depend substantially on circumstances outside individual control. [Original executive summary](https://www.oecdbetterlifeindex.org/media/bli/documents/EXECUTIVE-SUMMARY.pdf). The old index homepage now redirects to the [OECD Well-being Data Monitor](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/tools/well-being-data-monitor.html), which describes 11 current-well-being dimensions plus resources for future well-being. |

**Version distinction:** An older Full Focus article lists ten domains: spiritual, intellectual, emotional, physical, relational, parental, social, vocational, avocational, financial. The current assessment lists nine. Do not blend the versions or present ten as the current product taxonomy. [Older article, published 2021](https://fullfocusplanner.com/how-to-find-the-balance-you-need-for-the-life-you-want/).

## Recommended derivation

The following is a **design synthesis**, not a taxonomy endorsed by any source. Start with SAMHSA's broad coverage, use everyday names similar to Full Focus, preserve Stanford's explicit customization, and use OECD to check for omitted living conditions. A reasonable optional starter menu is:

| Proposed label | Source basis / deliberate adaptation |
| --- | --- |
| Physical health | SAMHSA physical; Full Focus body |
| Mental and emotional well-being | SAMHSA emotional; Full Focus mind |
| Relationships and belonging | SAMHSA social; Full Focus love/family/community; DYL love |
| Work and contribution | SAMHSA occupational; DYL work; “contribution” broadens the wording beyond employment |
| Money | SAMHSA financial; Full Focus money; OECD income |
| Learning and growth | SAMHSA intellectual; OECD education |
| Home and surroundings | SAMHSA environmental; OECD housing/environment; combines distinct scales for a starter label |
| Play and rest | DYL play; Full Focus hobbies; OECD work-life balance; “rest” is an added product prompt |
| Meaning and spirituality | SAMHSA spiritual; Full Focus spirit; wording permits secular interpretation |

Allow additions, renaming, splitting, merging, and hiding. Offer prompts for **partner/intimacy, family/caregiving, friends/community, civic participation, and safety** when the broad menu fails to capture the person's priorities. These are optional refinements, not assumed roles or deficits. Parent/partner categories must not imply that everyone has or wants those roles.

Store the person's own labels and definitions. An area can be satisfying, maintained, irrelevant, or deferred without acquiring an improvement goal. Treat balance and satisfaction as reflection questions rather than demanding equal attention or equal scores. These are product recommendations, not empirical findings from the comparison.

## Retrieval limits

Original organizational/author pages were used. The web reader returned errors opening the SAMHSA and Stanford PDFs directly; their primary-domain indexed text supplied the listed categories and Stanford's customization instruction. SAMHSA's official video and the authors' workbook page independently corroborate their respective category sets. No outcome-validation studies or comparative usability trials were reviewed.
